## Diagnosis: Full job crash — all NCCL processes exited simultaneously across the cluster

**Evidence:**
- **Telemetry pipeline is healthy** — `otel-collector` and all core scrape targets are UP (only the expected `vllm-*` targets are down, which is normal).
- **Collective bytes counter (`nccl_profiler_collective_bytes_total`) is completely flat right now** for all 4 ranks (rank 0/1 on bravo, rank 2/3 on golf) — first == last over the last 2 minutes.
- **`--find-anomaly` over 15 minutes pinpoints the stop to ~12:08:03–12:08:18**, where all 4 ranks' byte-counter rate dropped to exactly 0.00/s and never recovered.
- **GPU SM clocks on all 4 GPUs (both hosts) dropped from ~2800 MHz to the idle floor (180 MHz) at the same ~12:08 timestamp**, and remain there now (confirmed with a fresh 1-minute check, ratio ~1.0 across all ranks — i.e. uniformly idle, not one GPU clamped relative to healthy peers).
- **The smoking gun: `namedprocess_namegroup_num_procs{groupname="nccl-tests"}` dropped from 2→0 on *both* bravo and golf at 12:08:03–12:08:18.** The actual training processes exited on both hosts within about 15 seconds of each other.

**What this rules out:**
- Not a dead collector (it's up, and other metrics are genuinely frozen, not just stale).
- Not a single dead rank (all 4 stopped together).
- Not a per-GPU clock clamp/throttle (all 4 GPUs idled uniformly — that's the *consequence* of no workload running, not a power/thermal limit on one card).
- Not a network/interconnect degradation (there's no data moving to be degraded — the processes themselves are gone).

**Bottom line:** around 12:08 today, the NCCL training processes on both `bravo` and `golf` terminated at essentially the same moment (all 4 ranks of a 4-rank all-reduce job). That process exit is why collectives stopped and why GPU clocks fell to idle — both are downstream symptoms of the same event. This looks like a synchronized crash/exit of the job (e.g., a collective failure, an OOM, or an external kill) rather than a hardware or network fault. I'd recommend checking the training job's stderr/exit logs around 12:08:03–12:08:18 on both hosts for the actual crash reason, since the metrics available here can tell you *that* and *when* it died, but not *why*.
